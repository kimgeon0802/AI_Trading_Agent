# Loss Aversion

## Core Principle
Loss aversion is the psychological phenomenon where the pain of losing is psychologically about twice as powerful as the pleasure of gaining.

## Analysis Relevance
- Explains why investors hold onto losing stocks for too long ('disposition effect').

## Decision Guidelines
- Ensure that stop-loss or exit decisions are based on objective strategy, not on the emotional reluctance to 'realize a loss'.
- Treat a loss as part of the cost of doing business, not a personal failure.

## Strategy Validation Signals
- The ability to cut a losing trade quickly validates disciplined risk management.

## Risk Signals
- Holding losing positions, hoping for a 'break-even', significantly increases the risk of deeper capital loss.

## Common Misinterpretations
- Believing that a stock loss is not 'real' until it is sold.
- Confusing loss aversion with the investment thesis.

## Anti-Hallucination Rules
- Do NOT fabricate reasons to 'hold' a losing position.
- Do NOT analyze the agent's own emotional state (it has none).
