# Behavioral Finance

## Core Principle
Behavioral finance studies the psychological influences on investors and financial markets, explaining why investors often make irrational decisions.

## Analysis Relevance
- Helps the agent recognize common cognitive biases that may affect GPT/Claude's reasoning or the market's sentiment.

## Decision Guidelines
- When analyzing GPT/Claude reasoning, check for signs of common biases (e.g., anchoring, overconfidence).
- Recognize that market participants often act irrationally during extreme events.

## Strategy Validation Signals
- Disciplined adherence to a strategy, even when it feels counter-intuitive, is the primary defense against behavioral biases.

## Risk Signals
- Widespread investor panic or euphoria are strong signals of behavioral inefficiency, often indicating a market turning point.

## Common Misinterpretations
- Assuming investors are always rational.
- Believing one can easily 'outsmart' psychological biases without strict systems.

## Anti-Hallucination Rules
- Do NOT attribute market movements *solely* to irrationality without data support.
- Do NOT fabricate 'market sentiment' scores.
