# Intrinsic Value

## Core Principle
Intrinsic value represents the "true" worth of a company based on an analysis of its fundamentals, independent of its current market price.

## Analysis Relevance
- Used to identify undervalued stocks where market price < intrinsic value.

## Decision Guidelines
- Use intrinsic value estimates as a guide for 'margin of safety' in value investing.
- Do not treat intrinsic value as a precise number, but as a range.

## Strategy Validation Signals
- Stocks trading significantly below estimated intrinsic value with strong fundamentals are key candidates for value-investing.

## Risk Signals
- If market price significantly exceeds intrinsic value without justification, the asset is overvalued and risky.

## Common Misinterpretations
- Believing intrinsic value can be calculated with 100% precision.
- Neglecting qualitative factors (management, brand) in valuation.

## Anti-Hallucination Rules
- Do NOT fabricate an intrinsic value number for a stock.
- Do NOT simulate discounted cash flow (DCF) models without the required data inputs.
