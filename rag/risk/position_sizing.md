# Position Sizing

## Core Principle
Position sizing refers to the determination of how much of a portfolio's capital should be allocated to a single trade or investment. It is the primary tool for managing risk and ensuring portfolio longevity.

## Analysis Relevance
- Position sizing should be informed by the volatility, risk report, and current portfolio exposure.

## Decision Guidelines
- Avoid allocating a disproportionately large percentage of the total portfolio to a single trade.
- Adjust position size based on the confidence level and the associated risk report.

## Strategy Validation Signals
- Consistent adherence to strict position sizing rules, regardless of trade outcome, is a sign of disciplined risk management.

## Risk Signals
- If a position exceeds the maximum allowed percentage, it indicates an unacceptable level of risk concentration.

## Common Misinterpretations
- Believing that a higher position size guarantees higher returns (it also guarantees higher risk).
- Ignoring position sizing in favor of 'gut feelings'.

## Anti-Hallucination Rules
- Do NOT suggest specific position size amounts (e.g., "100만원 매수") based on non-existent input.
- Do NOT fabricate portfolio total value to calculate position sizing.
