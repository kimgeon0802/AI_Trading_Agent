# Drawdown

## Core Principle
A drawdown is the peak-to-trough decline during a specific period for an investment, trading account, or fund. It is a key measure of downside risk, representing the potential loss a position or portfolio could experience.

## Analysis Relevance
- Use historical drawdown data (if available in logs) to assess the potential risk of a current holding.
- Evaluate the maximum potential loss before a 'stop-loss' or exit decision is triggered.

## Decision Guidelines
- If a stock is currently experiencing a significant drawdown, assess if it is a temporary volatility or a fundamental change.
- Do not chase 'falling knives' based solely on the hope of recovery from a large drawdown.

## Strategy Validation Signals
- Positions that recover quickly from moderate drawdowns demonstrate stronger trend-following or value-investing potential.

## Risk Signals
- A drawdown exceeding predefined risk limits is a strong signal for immediate review or risk reduction.

## Common Misinterpretations
- Confusing a 'correction' with a 'reversal' during a drawdown.
- Believing that a deep drawdown is a guaranteed buying opportunity.

## Anti-Hallucination Rules
- Do NOT fabricate historical drawdown percentages for a stock.
- Do NOT predict the bottom of a drawdown.
- Do NOT create hypothetical maximum loss figures without actual input data.
