# Diversification

## Core Principle
Diversification is the practice of spreading investments across various financial instruments, industries, and other categories to reduce exposure to any single asset or risk. It aims to maximize returns by investing in different areas that would each react differently to the same event.

## Analysis Relevance
- When analyzing a stock, check if the proposed decision aligns with current portfolio holdings.
- Evaluate whether adding a position increases sector-specific or market-wide risk concentration.

## Decision Guidelines
- Do not add positions that create excessive concentration in one sector without a clear strategic reason.
- Evaluate the correlation between the target stock and existing portfolio assets.

## Strategy Validation Signals
- Consistent performance across different sectors often validates a well-diversified portfolio strategy.

## Risk Signals
- High concentration in a single stock or sector significantly increases the risk of a single negative event causing large portfolio drawdowns.

## Common Misinterpretations
- Assuming that more stocks automatically equate to better diversification (it depends on the correlation of those stocks).
- Believing diversification eliminates all risk (it reduces unsystematic risk, not systematic/market risk).

## Anti-Hallucination Rules
- Do NOT fabricate portfolio balance metrics.
- Do NOT assume the agent has knowledge of the user's entire external financial situation.
- Do NOT make diversification decisions based on unverified portfolio data.
