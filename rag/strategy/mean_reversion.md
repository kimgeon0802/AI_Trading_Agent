# Mean Reversion

## Core Principle
Mean reversion is the theory that asset prices and historical returns eventually return to the long-term mean or average level of the entire dataset.

## Analysis Relevance
- Useful for identifying potential entry points when a stock is significantly overextended from its historical average.

## Decision Guidelines
- Look for stocks that have moved significantly away from their moving averages and are showing signs of exhaustion.
- Do not assume a reversal *must* happen quickly; mean reversion can take time.

## Strategy Validation Signals
- Consistent reversals from oversold/overbought levels validate mean-reversion strategies.

## Risk Signals
- If a stock continues to trend away from the mean (momentum dominates), mean-reversion attempts can result in heavy losses.

## Common Misinterpretations
- Believing mean reversion is a guarantee for all stocks.
- Failing to account for fundamental structural changes in a company (the 'mean' may have changed).

## Anti-Hallucination Rules
- Do NOT fabricate the 'mean' price or average value.
- Do NOT predict the exact timing of a reversion.
