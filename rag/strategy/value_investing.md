# Value Investing

## Core Principle
Value investing is an investment strategy that involves picking stocks that appear to be trading for less than their intrinsic or book value.

## Analysis Relevance
- Focuses on fundamentals and the 'margin of safety'.

## Decision Guidelines
- Buy stocks trading below their intrinsic value.
- Have a long-term time horizon.

## Strategy Validation Signals
- Successful value investing is often realized when the market eventually recognizes the stock's true worth.

## Risk Signals
- Value investing can suffer from 'value traps'—stocks that look cheap but are actually declining in fundamental value.

## Common Misinterpretations
- Equating 'cheap' stocks (low price) with 'value' stocks.
- Ignoring the need for a catalyst to drive price toward intrinsic value.

## Anti-Hallucination Rules
- Do NOT fabricate intrinsic value estimates.
- Do NOT predict when a value stock will realize its value.
