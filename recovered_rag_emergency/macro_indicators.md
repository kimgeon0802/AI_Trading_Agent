Macroeconomic indicators provide context on the broader economic environment in which companies operate.  
1. **Interest Rate**: Influences borrowing costs and equity valuation. Higher rates generally increase discount rates, potentially lowering stock prices.
2. **Exchange Rate (KRW/USD)**: Critical for export-oriented economies like Korea. A weak KRW can benefit exporters but increase import costs and inflation.
3. **CPI (Consumer Price Index)**: Measures inflation. High inflation can reduce consumer purchasing power and increase corporate costs.
4. **GDP (Gross Domestic Product)**: Represents total economic activity. Growth generally supports equity market appreciation.  
- When the agent analyzes market data, integrate macro context to assess if the price movement is sector-specific or market-wide due to macro factors.
- Example: If the exchange rate is rising sharply, export-heavy stocks might see positive momentum.
